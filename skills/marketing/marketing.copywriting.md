# Marketing — Words That Make People Act, Not Just Read

## Core Philosophy

Copy exists for one reason: to move the reader to the next action. Every sentence earns its place by advancing that goal or it gets cut. Clever writing that doesn't convert is decoration. Boring writing that converts is product.

The cardinal sin of developer tool copy: describing what the tool does instead of what the user gets. Features are the mechanism. Benefits are the outcome. Users buy outcomes.

---

## When to Activate

- Writing landing page headlines, subheadlines, body copy
- Crafting CTAs that need to outperform generic "Sign Up" buttons
- Rewriting weak product descriptions or feature lists
- Creating urgency without false claims
- Email subject lines and push notifications

---

## Principles

**1. AIDA — the only framework you need.** Attention (stop the scroll), Interest (here's something relevant to you), Desire (imagine this being yours), Action (here's how to get it). Every piece of copy follows this sequence or fails.

**2. Specific beats vague, always.** "Faster" means nothing. "Loads in 0.4 seconds" is a claim someone can believe or disprove. Specificity signals honesty. Vagueness signals you don't know your numbers.

**3. One CTA per page section.** Every additional CTA you add cuts the probability of clicking any of them. Choose one action. Make it unavoidable.

**4. Power words activate emotion.** "Free", "proven", "instant", "guaranteed", "you", "new", "secret", "easy". These words have outsized psychological weight. Use them when they're true.

**5. Inverted pyramid — lead with the conclusion.** The most important claim goes first. Details follow. Readers leave after the first sentence more often than you think. If the most important thing is buried in paragraph 3, no one reads it.

**6. Scarcity and urgency only when real.** "Only 3 spots left" when there are 3000 is a lie. Readers detect it and trust collapses permanently. Real scarcity: beta cohort of 200, early access closes Friday, founding price until launch.

## Decision Framework

```
What is the ONE thing this copy must communicate?
└── Write that. Cut everything else that doesn't support it.

Who reads this and what do they already believe?
├── Developer → skeptical, hates hype, respects specifics and code
├── Executive → time-poor, wants ROI and risk reduction
└── Consumer → emotional, wants identity and outcome, not features

What is the next action after reading?
├── Click → CTA must name the action + the value: "Get Free Access"
├── Subscribe → lead with what they get, not that they're subscribing
└── Share → give them a reason (identity signal, exclusive information)

Is there a weak "it" or "this" in the headline?
├── YES → replace with the actual noun — "It saves time" → "EffiKit cuts review time by 60%"
└── NO  → check for passive voice and eliminate it
```

---

## Anti-Patterns

- "We are a leading provider of..." — nobody reads past "we are"
- Feature lists without outcomes — "10 components" means nothing without "ship in hours, not days"
- "Learn more" as CTA — learn what? about what? be specific
- Adjective inflation: "powerful", "robust", "seamless", "innovative" — delete all of them
- Writing for yourself — "we're excited to announce" → the reader doesn't care about your excitement
- Buried CTA — if they have to scroll to find how to act, conversion drops 30-40%

## Example in Action

Rewrite weak developer tool headline into 3 converting variants:

**Original (weak):**
> "A powerful toolkit for modern developers"

**Why it fails:** "powerful" is meaningless, "modern" is not a benefit, "toolkit" is generic. Zero specificity. Could describe any product built since 2010.

---

**Variant 1 — Outcome-first:**
> "Ship production-ready features in the time it takes to write a Jira ticket."

- Addresses: developer frustration with slow delivery
- Mechanism implied: pre-built, ready-to-use
- Tone: irreverent, developer-native

---

**Variant 2 — Specificity + social proof:**
> "300+ TypeScript utilities. Zero boilerplate. Used by 1,200 developers in production."

- Three concrete claims, all verifiable
- "Zero boilerplate" hits the primary pain point
- Numbers give credibility without explanation

---

**Variant 3 — Enemy narrative:**
> "Stop writing the same fetch wrapper for the 11th time."

- Names the specific enemy (repeated boilerplate)
- "11th time" is specific enough to feel true
- Implies: this solves exactly that
- Tone: solidarity with the developer's experience

**CTA upgrade:**
- Weak: "Get Started" 
- Strong: "Grab the Toolkit — It's Free"
- Stronger: "Copy Your First Utility in 30 Seconds"
