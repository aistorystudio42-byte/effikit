# Marketing — Own a Category, Not Just a Feature

## Core Philosophy

Positioning is not what you say about your product. It is what users think of the moment they hear your name. You don't control that directly — but you control the frame. The frame is set before the user ever tries the product, through every word, comparison, and story you tell.

The fatal mistake: entering an existing category and competing on features. Feature wars are won by incumbents with more resources. Category creation is won by whoever shows up first with the right frame.

---

## When to Activate

- Launching or re-launching a product
- Writing the homepage tagline and positioning statement
- Deciding which competitors to name and which to ignore
- Pricing conversations (price signals category)
- Any time "it's like X but better" is the pitch

---

## Principles

**1. Category design beats category entry.** "Another project management tool" is a death sentence. "The first tool designed specifically for solo developers who hate standups" is a category. You are the only member of a category you create, so you are automatically the leader.

**2. The positioning statement is a constraint, not a slogan.** Format: "For [specific customer] who [specific problem], [product] is the [category] that [unique differentiation] because [proof]." Everything else flows from this.

**3. Competitive moat comes from what you make impossible.** A feature is copyable in 3 months. An integration ecosystem, network effect, or data moat takes years to replicate. Identify yours and build every message around it.

**4. Messaging hierarchy: category → differentiation → features.** Never lead with features. Lead with what kind of thing this is (category), then why this version is different (differentiation), then what it specifically does (features). Inverting this order is the most common positioning error.

**5. Feature-first positioning is a trap.** "We have 50 templates" is not a position. "The tool that learns how your team works" is a position. One describes outputs, the other describes a promise.

---

## Decision Framework

```
Is there a named category this product fits into?
├── YES → do you have a realistic path to #1 or #2 in that category?
│   ├── YES → enter the category, compete on differentiation
│   └── NO  → define a sub-category where you can lead
└── NO  → create the category — name it, define it, own it

Who is the specific enemy?
├── Identifiable competitor → you can contrast against them by name
├── A behavior/habit (not a company) → "spreadsheet thinking" as the enemy
└── A belief → "the idea that good tools must be complex"

What does the customer already believe?
├── Map existing beliefs → position alongside, not against them
└── Changing beliefs costs 10× more than confirming them
```

---

## Anti-Patterns

- "Best-in-class" — superlatives without proof are noise
- Competing in a category dominated by a company with 100× your budget
- Trying to be the tool for everyone — "any developer" is not a positioning
- Changing positioning every 6 months — trust is built on consistency
- Letting features define the category instead of the customer's problem
- Naming the competition in a way that makes them look bigger than you
- Pricing below the category leader — signals lower quality, not better value

## Example in Action

Position Effikit against ChatGPT and Cursor AI:

**The wrong way — feature comparison:**
> "Unlike ChatGPT, Effikit gives you structured utilities. Unlike Cursor, it works without an IDE."

This makes ChatGPT and Cursor the reference point. Effikit becomes the alternative to something, which is always a weaker position.

---

**The right way — category creation:**

**Category name:** Developer Context System  
(Not an AI tool, not a snippet library — a system that makes every AI session more effective)

**Positioning statement:**
> "For developers who use AI coding assistants daily, Effikit is the context layer that turns vague AI responses into production-ready output because it gives the AI exactly what it needs to reason like a senior engineer."

**Messaging hierarchy for homepage:**

1. **Category (what it is):**  
   "The system that makes your AI assistant actually useful."

2. **Differentiation (why this one):**  
   "Not prompts. Not templates. A structured knowledge base your AI reads before every task."

3. **Features (how it works):**  
   - 300+ TypeScript utilities it can pull from directly  
   - Skill directives that shape how it thinks  
   - Algorithm library for decisions it can't fake

**Competitive frame:**
- vs. ChatGPT: ChatGPT is the engine. Effikit is the fuel.
- vs. Cursor: Cursor is the IDE. Effikit is the expertise.
- vs. GitHub Copilot: Copilot autocompletes. Effikit architectures.

**Price as signal:**  
Free and open-source positions Effikit as infrastructure (you don't charge for your toolbox), which differentiates from SaaS AI tools that charge monthly. This is itself a positioning statement.
